# GRS Benchmark Results (Re 4 sub-goal 5, 2026-05-27)

## Background

The "GRS" benchmarks (`20230224-grsbits-truby` in SMT-LIB 2023) verify
floating-point multiplication using a word-level bit-vector
representation. Generated by Robin Trüby, Mathias Fleury, and Armin
Biere; target solver Bitwuzla. The naming convention is
`grs-<exponent-size>-<mantissa-size>.smt2`. There are 20 benchmarks
spanning exponent ∈ {32, 64, 96, 128} and mantissa ∈ {16, 32, 64, 96, 128}.

The benchmarks are universal-equational queries (`a + b = b + a` style
identities under the GRS rounding model) over wide bit-vectors (up to
the sum of exponent and mantissa bitwidths, e.g., 256 bits for
grs-128-128). Each benchmark uses primarily `bvshl`, `bvlshr`,
`bvadd`, `bvsub`, `bvuge` operations, with a relatively low count of
each (no `bvmul`).

The paper's §4.6 (current state) reports CBMC 3, Bitwuzla 4, cvc5 0
at a 30s timeout, with Bitwuzla "uniquely solving grs-128-16".

## Diagnosis: why the improvement?

Re 4 (bit-decomposition variables for `bvlshr`, etc.) is **not** the
primary driver of the GRS improvement. Verified by running grs-128-16
with `DISABLE_ALGEBRAIC=1` (pure bit-blasting baseline): solves in
37.05 s, essentially the same as our default (37.46 s). The
algebraic layer is invisible on these queries (no `bvmul` / no
polynomial structure to find).

The GRS improvement (3/20 → 7/20 from 30 s to 60 s) is principally
about **timeout**: Bitwuzla and CBMC's bit-blasting backend both
solve more GRS benchmarks given more time. The previous paper result
at 30 s reflected the budget more than capability differences.

## Implications for the paper

1. **§4.6 update**: the prior text "Bitwuzla uniquely solves
   grs-128-16" is no longer accurate at 60s timeout. We solve it
   too, in 37.5 s. At 30 s it remains Bitwuzla-only.

2. **At 60 s budget**: we exceed Bitwuzla on GRS (7 vs 6). At
   120 s budget: tied (8 vs 8) with disjoint extras (we get
   grs-32-64; they get grs-128-32).

3. **Honest framing**: the GRS improvement is from extended timeout
   exposing more bit-blasting capability, not from Re 4 specifically.
   These benchmarks have no polynomial structure for our algebraic
   layer to exploit (no `bvmul`).

4. **Actionable changes to §4.6**:
   - Update the totals to "smt2_solver 7, Bitwuzla 6, cvc5 1 at
     60 s (was 3/4/0 at 30 s)".
   - Remove "Bitwuzla uniquely solves grs-128-16" or qualify with
     "at 30 s timeout".
   - Add a sentence noting that the GRS benchmarks don't exercise
     `bvmul` and therefore don't benefit from our algebraic layer;
     the improvement on them reflects general bit-blasting
     performance.
