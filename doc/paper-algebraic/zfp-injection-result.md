# Item 6: ZFP injection into Gröbner basis — empirical evaluation

## Hypothesis (Martin)

Add zero-function-polynomial (ZFP) ideal generators to the input
basis of Buchberger. If this works, the resulting strong Gröbner
basis automatically reduces ZFPs to zero, and the separate §3
vanishing-polynomial test (Shekhar et al.~/~Gámez-Montolio et al.)
becomes redundant.

The univariate ZFP ideal in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^d}[x]$ is generated by
$2^{\max(d - \nu_2(k!), 0)} \cdot x^{\underline{k}}$ for
$k = 2, \ldots, \mathrm{SF}(2^d)$, where $x^{\underline{k}}$ is the
falling factorial.

## Implementation

- **`vanishing.{h,cpp}`**: new `generate_zfp_generators(d, var_idx,
  input_width)` that returns the univariate ZFP generators for one
  variable. Reuses existing `nu2`, `nu2_factorial`,
  `smarandache_function` helpers.
- **`boolbv.cpp`**: behind `ENABLE_ZFP_INJECTION=1`, injects ZFP
  generators for every input-symbol variable (skipping
  `__rabinowitsch`, `__fresh_mul_*`, etc.) into the Buchberger input
  basis. Both the per-disequality and the global Gröbner paths.
- Knobs: `ZFP_MIN_K` (default 2), `ZFP_MAX_K` (default = full SF) for
  selecting which generators to inject. `DISABLE_VANISHING=1`
  disables the §3 separate test.

## Experimental setup

Full 39-benchmark custom suite (Paper 2 Table 1, canonical list
from `doc/paper-algebraic/data/paper2-suite-results.tsv`). Four
configurations:

| Config         | Vanishing (§3) | ZFP injection |
|----------------|:---:|:---:|
| `default`      | on  | off |
| `vanish_off`   | off | off |
| `zfp_only`     | off | on  |
| `zfp_plus_van` | on  | on  |

30 s timeout per cell. CBMC `--multiplier-encoding comba-cs` with the
algebraic layer enabled.

## Results

```
default      : 39/39  <-- Paper 2 baseline
vanish_off   : 36/39  (loses 3: §3 needed for these)
zfp_only     : 30/39  <-- WORSE than vanish_off, loses 9
zfp_plus_van : 37/39  (REGRESSES on 2 vs default)
```

### Vanishing test is essential for 3 benchmarks

Three benchmarks T/O without the §3 vanishing test, regardless of
whether ZFP injection is on or off:

- `dsp_image_reject` (DSP datapath)
- `dsp_horner_16` (DSP datapath)
- `dsp_vanishing_mv` (multi-variate ZFP)

### ZFP injection causes 9 regressions

Compared to `default`, `zfp_only` *loses* 9 benchmarks. 6 of these
are benchmarks that don't even need the vanishing test (they solve
trivially without it):

| Benchmark            | default | vanish_off | zfp_only | zfp_plus_van |
|----------------------|---------|------------|----------|--------------|
| overflow_detect_16   | 0.00 s  | 0.00 s     | **T/O**  | 0.00 s       |
| add_overflow_16      | 0.00 s  | 0.00 s     | **11.4 s** | 0.00 s     |
| checked_mul_16       | 0.00 s  | 0.00 s     | **T/O**  | 0.00 s       |
| equiv_unsat_8add_16  | 0.00 s  | 0.00 s     | **T/O**  | 0.00 s       |
| crypto_square_mod    | 0.01 s  | 0.01 s     | **1.9 s** | **1.9 s**    |
| fixedpoint_mul_16    | 0.02 s  | 0.02 s     | **T/O**  | **T/O**      |
| mul_ineq_12          | 0.50 s  | 0.50 s     | **10.3 s** | **10.2 s** |
| dsp_image_reject     | 0.01 s  | T/O        | T/O      | 0.01 s       |
| dsp_horner_16        | 0.00 s  | T/O        | T/O      | 0.01 s       |
| dsp_vanishing_poly_8 | 0.00 s  | 20.89 s    | **T/O**  | 0.00 s       |
| dsp_vanishing_mv     | 0.00 s  | T/O        | T/O      | 0.00 s       |
| div_mul_roundtrip_12 | 3.97 s  | 3.98 s     | **T/O**  | **T/O**      |

**Most striking**: `add_overflow_16`, `checked_mul_16`,
`equiv_unsat_8add_16`, `crypto_square_mod`, `mul_ineq_12`,
`fixedpoint_mul_16`, `div_mul_roundtrip_12` are all benchmarks
that don't need ZFPs at all — they solve in milliseconds with
vanishing *off*. ZFP injection makes them 100×–unboundedly slower
or causes outright timeouts.

### `zfp_plus_van` regresses on 2 benchmarks vs `default`

Even adding ZFP injection on *top* of the working §3 test causes
new failures:

- `div_mul_roundtrip_12`: 3.97 s → T/O.
- `fixedpoint_mul_16`: 0.02 s → T/O.

These are not solvable by the §3 test alone (it doesn't apply); the
default behaviour relies on the standard Buchberger run. ZFP
injection bloats that basis enough that Buchberger now exceeds the
100k-step budget and the queries fall through to bit-blasting,
where they time out.

### Where ZFP injection helps

Zero benchmarks. There is no benchmark in the suite where
`zfp_only > vanish_off`. The only benchmark where
`vanish_off` T/Os and `zfp_only` solves… does not exist on the
custom suite.

## Why does Martin's hypothesis fail?

Adding ZFP ideal generators to the basis is **mathematically
correct** — Buchberger with these generators decides functional
equivalence over $\mathbb{Z}_{2^d}^n \to \mathbb{Z}_{2^d}$, not just
polynomial equivalence. But it's **algorithmically impractical** for
two reasons:

1. **Basis bloat.** For $d = 8$, SF$(2^d) = 10$, so we add 9 ZFP
   generators *per input variable*. Each has degree up to 10 and
   coefficients up to $2^{d-1}$. The S-polynomial pairs grow
   quadratically in the basis size. Even for benchmarks that don't
   need ZFPs (like `mul_no_overflow_16`), Buchberger now spends
   considerable time computing useless S-polynomials.

2. **SSA-induced indirection.** Most paper benchmarks use SSA-style
   `define-fun` for intermediate products (`x2 = x*x`, `x3 = x2*x`,
   etc.). To use a degree-10 ZFP, Buchberger must first reduce a
   polynomial in `x2, x3, ..., x10` to an inline polynomial in `x`
   alone — a long S-polynomial chain that hits the 100 000-step
   reduction budget. The §3 test sidesteps this by *substituting*
   SSA definitions before testing.

The §3 test of Shekhar-Kalla / Gámez-Montolio Algorithm 3 is
intrinsically more efficient because:
- It substitutes SSA definitions to get a single inline polynomial.
- It works *directly* on the falling-factorial basis (one-pass
  conversion via Stirling numbers).
- It checks the divisibility conditions on coefficients directly,
  without S-polynomial computation.

## Conclusion

**The hypothesis is empirically refuted.** ZFP injection does not
subsume the §3 vanishing-polynomial test; on the full
39-benchmark suite, it loses 9 benchmarks vs the default
configuration, including 7 benchmarks that don't even need ZFPs
to solve. Adding ZFP injection on *top* of the working vanishing
test causes 2 new regressions. The §3 test remains essential.

This is itself a useful contribution to Paper 2: it justifies the
two-phase architecture (Gröbner basis + §3 vanishing test) over the
naïve "add ZFPs to the basis" alternative one might propose.

## Recommendation for Paper 2

Add a paragraph to §3 (or to §5 Discussion) noting:

> One might ask whether the vanishing polynomial test could be
> subsumed by adding generators of the zero-function polynomial
> ideal directly to the input basis of Buchberger's algorithm. We
> implemented this experimentally and found it impractical: even
> with the SF$(2^d)$ falling-factorial generators added per variable,
> Buchberger fails to reduce non-trivial DSP benchmarks within
> 100,000 reduction steps because of the SSA-induced indirection
> (intermediate variables for nested multiplications), and the extra
> generators slow down even benchmarks that do not need ZFPs by
> three orders of magnitude. The §3 test sidesteps both issues by
> substituting SSA definitions and converting directly to the
> falling-factorial basis.
